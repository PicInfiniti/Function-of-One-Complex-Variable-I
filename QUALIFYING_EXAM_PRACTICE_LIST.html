Complex Analysis Qualifying Exam — Priority Practice List

This list is based on all 156 exercises in the repository:

  • 9 questions in Exams/main.tex;
  • 40 questions from the August 2018, August 2020, August 2024, and January 2025 qualifying exams;
  • 107 textbook exercises in Exercise/complex.tex.

The ordering gives the most weight to questions that appeared on a qualifying exam, especially when essentially the same textbook exercise later appeared on an exam. Check an item only after you can solve it from a blank page, justify every theorem's hypotheses, and explain the proof aloud.

If time is short — do these 12 first

  1. Prove the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra by completing the following two steps for a nonconstant polynomial \[ p(z)=a_0+a_1z+\cdots+a_nz^n, \] where \(a_n\neq 0\) and \(n\ge 1\).

    (a) Show that there is a nonzero polynomial \(q(z)\) such that \[ \frac{1}{zp(z)}=\frac{1}{zp(0)}+\frac{q(z)}{p(z)}, \] for \(z\neq 0\) and \(p(z)\neq 0\).
    (b) By means of integration over the circle \(C_R=Re^{it}\) with \(t\in[0,2\pi]\), show that \(p(z_0)=0\) for some point \(z_0\) in \(\mathbb{C}\).
    Source: January 2025, I.1

  2. How many roots does the polynomial \[ z^9+z^5-8z^3+2z+1 \] have between the circles \(\{|z|=1\}\) and \(\{|z|=2\}\)?

    Source: August 2024, I.3

  3. Show that the equation \[ e^z=cz, \] where \(c\) is a constant with \(|c|>e\), has exactly one solution in the set \[ \{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}z<1\}. \]

    Source: January 2025, I.2

  4. Suppose $f$ is a holomorphic function on the open unit disc \[ D=\{z:|z|<1\}. \] Prove that there is a sequence $(z_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$ in $D$ such that $|z_n|\to 1$ and the set \[ \{f(z_n):n\geq 1\} \] is bounded.

    Source: August 2018, I.3

  5. Show that if \(f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}\) is a continuous function such that \(f\) is analytic off \([-1,1]\), then \(f\) is an entire function.

    Source: January 2025, I.3

  6. Suppose that $F$ and $G$ are holomorphic functions on the right half plane \[ H=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}(z)>0\}. \] Suppose also that $F$ and $G$ have the same zeros with the same multiplicities at each zero. Prove that there is a holomorphic function $h$ on $H$ such that \[ F(z)=e^{h(z)}G(z) \] for all $z\in H$.

    Source: August 2018, I.2

  7. Find a conformal map \(f\) from the vertical strip \[ \Omega=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:3<\operatorname{Re}(z)<11\} \] onto the open unit disk \(\mathbb{D}\).

    Source: August 2018, I.1

  8. Let \[ G=\{z:\operatorname{Re}(z)>0\}, \] and \(f:G\to G\) be a one-one analytic function from \(G\) into \(G\). Suppose \(f(a)=a\) for some positive real number \(a\). Show that \[ |f'(a)|\le 1. \]

    Source: August 2020, II.4

  9. Let $\mathfrak{F}$ be the collection of holomorphic functions on the open unit disk \[ D=\{z:|z|<1\} \] satisfying \[ \iint_D |f|\,dx\,dy \leq 1. \] Prove or disprove that $\mathfrak{F}$ is a normal family.

    Source: August 2018, II.5

  10. Suppose that \(G\) is a region in \(\mathbb{C}\), \(\{f_n\}\) is a sequence in \(H(G)\), \(f\) is a nonconstant function, and \(f_n\to f\) in \(H(G)\). Let \(a\in G\) and \(b=f(a)\). Show that there is a sequence \(\{a_n\}\) in \(G\) such that

    (i) \(a=\lim a_n\),
    (ii) \(f_n(a_n)=b\) for sufficiently large \(n\).
    Source: August 2020, II.3

  11. Given entire functions \(f,g\) without common zeros, prove that there exist entire functions \(A,B\) such that \[ Af+Bg=1. \] Hint: Use the Mittag-Leffler Theorem.

    Source: August 2020, II.1

  12. Let \(G\) be a domain in the complex plane and \(u:G\to\mathbb{R}\) be a nonconstant harmonic function and let \[ A=\{z\in G:u_x(z)=u_y(z)=0\}. \] Show that \(A\) does not have any limit points in \(G\).

    Source: January 2025, II.2

Tier 1 — Must practice

1. Fundamental Theorem of Algebra by several methods

Why: the FTA appeared three times in four recorded qualifying exams, with three different requested proofs.

2. Rouché's theorem and counting zeros

Why: you should be able to choose the comparison function and contour yourself, not only recognize Rouché after being told to use it.

3. Liouville, growth, and maximum/minimum modulus arguments

4. Boundary blow-up contradiction on the disk

Why: this exact idea returned six years later. Know how to remove the finitely many zeros and apply the maximum modulus principle to the reciprocal.

5. Cauchy's formula, residues, and winding numbers

6. Removable sets and Morera's theorem

Why: be ready to subdivide triangles intersecting the segment and use continuity to control the thin pieces.

7. Analytic logarithms, zeros, and branches

8. Conformal maps and Möbius transformations

9. Schwarz lemma and half-plane self-maps

10. Normal families from estimates

Why: this requires converting an area integral into a uniform bound on compact subsets, typically through the mean-value property or Cauchy estimates.

11. Operations on normal families

12. Hurwitz-type convergence and moving points

13. Weierstrass products and prescribed zeros

14. Mittag-Leffler and analytic Bézout identities

15. Basic analyticity, inverse functions, and reflection

16. Open mapping and restricted ranges

17. Harmonic functions

Tier 2 — Important advanced coverage

18. Infinite-product functional equations

19. Local geometry at a double zero

20. Polynomial approximation versus compact convergence

21. Laurent series and isolated singularities

22. Residues for real integrals

23. Boundary modulus and finite Blaschke products

24. Riemann mapping and topology of punctures

25. Subharmonic functions

26. Runge and approximation obstructions

27. Phragmén–Lindelöf

Lower-priority warm-ups

Use these for speed, but do not spend your main study time on them before completing Tier 1:

  • Calculate the following:

    (1) the square roots of $i$
    (2) the cube roots of $i$
    (3) the square roots of $\sqrt{3} + 3i$
    Source: Exercise/complex.tex, line 112

  • If $(X,d)$ is any metric space show that every open ball is an open set. Also, show that every closed ball is a closed set.

    Source: Exercise/complex.tex, line 269

  • Let $\gamma$ and $\sigma$ be the two polygons $[1,i]$ and $[1,\,1+i,\,i]$. Express $\gamma$ and $\sigma$ as paths and calculate $\int_\gamma f$ and $\int_\sigma f$ where $f(z)=|z|^{2}$.

    Source: Exercise/complex.tex, line 1588

  • Find the radius of convergence for each of the following power series:

    (1) $\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a^n z^n, \quad a \in \mathbb{C};$
    (2) $\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a^{n^2} z^n, \quad a \in \mathbb{C};$
    (3) $\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} k^n z^n, \quad k \text{ an integer } \neq 0;$
    (4) $\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} z^{n!}.$
    Source: Exercise/complex.tex, line 860

  • Show that if $F_1$ and $F_2$ are primitives for $f:G\to\mathbb{C}$ and $G$ is open and connected, then there is a constant $c$ such that $F_1(z)=c+F_2(z)$ for each $z\in G$.

    Source: Exercise/complex.tex, line 1747

Recommended order

  1. First pass: items 1–9 (core first-semester techniques).
  2. Second pass: items 10–17 (convergence, factorization, and harmonic theory).
  3. Third pass: items 18–27 (Part II and advanced topics).
  4. Final review: redo every qualifying-exam question above under a 20–25 minute limit without notes.

A solution counts as exam-ready only if you can state exactly why the relevant hypotheses hold—for example, why a quotient has removable singularities, why a logarithm exists on the domain, which zeros lie inside a Rouché contour, or why a family is locally bounded.
